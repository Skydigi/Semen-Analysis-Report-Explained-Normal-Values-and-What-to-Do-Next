# Understanding Your Semen Analysis Report

A simple guide to reading a semen analysis: what the values mean, what affects them, and what to do next.

> ⚠️ **Medical note:** This guide is for general education only. A fertility specialist or urologist should interpret your results, together with your medical history, an examination and any other tests you've had. WHO reference limits are statistical values. They don't draw a line between fertile and infertile.

---

## What Is It?

A semen analysis checks how much sperm there is and how healthy it is. It's a quick, non-invasive test and often the first one done for the male partner.

- **Collection:** By masturbation into a sterile container, usually at the clinic.
- **Abstinence:** Usually 2–7 days without ejaculation beforehand.
- **Home collection:** Only if the lab allows it. The sample usually needs to reach the lab within about 1 hour.

---

## WHO Reference Values (6th Edition, 2021)

| Parameter | Lower Reference Limit | Measures |
|---|---|---|
| Semen volume | 1.4 mL | Amount of fluid |
| Sperm concentration | 16 million/mL | Sperm per mL |
| Total sperm number | 39 million/ejaculate | Total sperm |
| Total motility | 42% | Sperm that move |
| Progressive motility | 30% | Sperm moving forward |
| Normal morphology | 4% | Sperm with normal shape (strict criteria) |
| Vitality | 54% | Live sperm |
| pH | ≥ 7.2 | Acidity/alkalinity |

These limits are not a pass/fail fertility test. The overall pattern, repeat tests and both partners' results all matter.

---

## Common Terms

- **Oligozoospermia:** Low sperm concentration
- **Asthenozoospermia:** Reduced motility
- **Teratozoospermia:** Fewer normally shaped sperm
- **OAT:** All three of the above together
- **Azoospermia:** No sperm seen in the sample (needs further evaluation)
- **Leukocytes / pus cells:** May point to inflammation or infection (may need confirmatory tests)

---

## One Bad Report ≠ Final Answer

Sperm take about **2–3 months** to develop. Fever, illness, heat or medication changes during that time can affect a result. Results also vary from sample to sample, so an abnormal test is often repeated. Follow your specialist's advice on when.

---

## What Affects Sperm Quality?

- Smoking, tobacco and excess alcohol
- Obesity and poorly controlled diabetes
- Heat exposure
- Varicocele
- Infections and past illnesses
- Hormonal disorders (thyroid, testosterone)
- Some medicines and anabolic steroids
- Genetic or structural conditions

> Don't stop prescribed medicines or start supplements without talking to your doctor.

---

## Possible Next Steps

| Finding | Possible Approach |
|---|---|
| Mild abnormalities | Lifestyle changes, medication review, treating infection, varicocele evaluation |
| Suitable selected cases | **IUI**: prepared sperm placed in the uterus around ovulation |
| Significant abnormalities | **IVF ± ICSI**: a single sperm injected into an egg |
| Azoospermia | Tests to find the cause; surgical sperm retrieval + ICSI in selected cases |

Further tests may include hormone tests, an examination, scrotal ultrasound, genetic testing or DNA fragmentation testing. Not everyone needs these.

---

## FAQ

**What sperm count is "normal"?**
The WHO lower limits are 16 million/mL and 39 million per ejaculate. Pregnancy can still happen below these numbers.

**Is 2% morphology normal?**
It's below the 4% limit, but morphology alone doesn't decide fertility.

**Can sperm count improve naturally?**
Sometimes. Quitting smoking, managing weight and diabetes, and avoiding steroids can help. Changes take about 3 months to show up in a test.
